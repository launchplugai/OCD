## Winning Product Structure

- **Name:** **The OCD Protocol™ — Obsession-to-Catalog Operating System**
- **Proprietary mechanism:** the **Entropy Filter** — a three-stage triage (Obsession → Curation → Distribution) that decides, per track, whether it ships, gets cannibalized for parts, or dies.
- **Contents:**
  1. *The Protocol* — ~50-page playbook covering each stage of the Entropy Filter, written in the same voice as the OCD Records site.
  2. *The Notion OS* — duplicate-able template with the Entropy Filter board, release calendar, splits tracker, sync-readiness checklist.
  3. *The Submission Kit* — pre-formatted one-sheets and metadata templates aimed at OCD Records + ~12 sibling labels, plus the exact wording that gets demos opened.
  4. *Licensing Readiness Checklist* — stems naming, ISRC/ISWC, splits doc, sync-cleared metadata; mirrors what Tier 01–03 buyers on `licensing.html` actually need.
  5. *Three Deconstructions* — case studies of signed releases (start with Entropy 001 / Neural Ghost) showing the Entropy Filter applied in retrospect.
- **Delivery (3am-safe):** Gumroad or Stan Store. Single checkout → instant download + Notion duplicate link → 7-email onboarding sequence (ConvertKit). No calls, no Zoom, no Slack.
- **Price point:** **$97**. Rationale: roughly 1/8 the price of one Premium sync license on `licensing.html` ($799), low enough for an obsessive producer's impulse buy, high enough to filter tire-kickers and fund paid acquisition. Premium tier at $297 adds the Catalog Vault first month and a Loom-recorded one-time Entropy Filter audit of the buyer's project folder (still async — no live presence).
- **What makes it irreplaceable:** the methodology is **back-derived from a real working label's intake and licensing pipeline** — not a producer-influencer's hypothesis. The case studies reference *signed* releases on the site, which competitors can't fake.

## Distribution Channel (matches existing specific knowledge)

**The OCD Records submissions inbox + site itself.** Every demo submission on `submit.html` is already a perfectly qualified lead: a bedroom producer with an unfinished track who wants on a label. Two zero-new-platform moves:

1. **Auto-responder on every submission:** "We review every demo. While you wait — here's the Protocol we use to decide who gets signed." Link to the product. This converts the highest-intent traffic the project already attracts.
2. **A "The Protocol" nav entry** on `index.html` (between Catalog and Studios) so existing site traffic encounters the product without changing channel strategy.

No new TikTok, no new Substack, no cold outreach. Same audience, same site, same brand voice.
