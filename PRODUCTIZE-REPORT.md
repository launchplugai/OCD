## 1. Why this works: the three operators you were told to study

### Naval Ravikant — "Productize Yourself"

Naval's whole thesis is that the internet lets one person package *specific knowledge* into code or media and sell it without showing up. Two phrases matter:

- **Specific knowledge** = something that can't be taught in a classroom because it's earned through obsession. OCD's A&R taste qualifies. A *general* "how to release music" course does not.
- **Permissionless leverage** = code and content. Both work while you sleep. Live coaching does not — that's *labor leverage* and it caps your income at your hours.

The Vault uses media (PDF + scorecards) and digital assets (stems + presets) — both permissionless. The Fast-Pass Loom review is the only labor in the system, and it's capped at one batch per month.

### Dan Koe — "One person, one offer, one channel"

Koe's lesson is that complexity kills solo operators. He preaches:

- **One offer that solves one painful problem.** The Vault solves: *"I have no idea if my track is good enough for a real label."*
- **One distribution channel you already have leverage on.** You have the submission funnel. Don't start a TikTok.
- **A worldview, not just a product.** Your worldview is "obsession becomes art" and "neural precision serves intuition." Every piece of copy, the PDF, even the Loom intro should breathe that.

### Alex Hormozi — "Grand slam offer + value stack"

Hormozi's framework is to make the offer so good people feel stupid saying no. The Vault checks his four levers:

1. **Dream outcome:** "Get graded by OCD's actual A&R rubric — without the rejection email."
2. **Perceived likelihood of success:** real anonymized scorecards inside the Vault *prove* the system works.
3. **Time delay:** instant download; Loom returned within 30 days.
4. **Effort & sacrifice:** no sales call, no cohort, no course schedule. Buy, download, upload your track, get the scorecard.

He'd also approve of the **$197 price point** — high enough that buyers pay attention, low enough that nobody needs a payment plan.

### Why A wins (and B and C lose)

**Why B (Sound Mirror) loses on feasibility:** building a useful AI grader in 30 days means renting compute, fighting hallucinations, and learning audio analysis pipelines. You'd ship a brittle MVP. The Vault uses zero new infrastructure — Gumroad + a PDF + a Dropbox folder.

**Why C (Cohort) loses on leverage:** "monthly curator notes" sounds async, but the moment you have 200 students it becomes a part-time job. Cohorts drift toward live work. The Vault caps fulfillment at one Loom per buyer per *year*.

**Why A wins on all three axes:**
- **Leverage 9/10** — only the Loom batch (90 minutes once a month, in your pajamas) requires you.
- **Feasibility 9/10** — you already have the stems, the taste, and the website's design system. Writing the playbook is the only real lift.
- **Margin 10/10** — Gumroad fee ~5%, artist rev share 20%, fulfillment ~$0. Roughly **75% net margin even after paying artists**.

**The Artifact Vault — powered by the Neural Precision Protocol™ (NPP)**

You need both halves of the name. "Vault" tells buyers *what they get*. "NPP" tells them *what makes it irreplaceable*. Trademark or not, name it like it's proprietary — because it is.

That 5× value-to-price ratio is the Hormozi sweet spot.

### The 7 stages of the Neural Precision Protocol

You already do these. The product is just naming them.

1. **Signal Audit** — does the track have a non-negotiable idea worth preserving?
2. **Frequency Map** — spectral fingerprint vs. OCD catalog DNA.
3. **Intuition Pass** — human-only listen, no meters, no notes, just gut.
4. **Neural Pass** — structured AI/reference critique against a labeled set.
5. **Tension Calibration** — arrangement scoring rubric (build / drop / decay).
6. **Artifact Finish** — mix and master gates that must hit before release.
7. **Sync-Readiness Score** — license-clearance checklist (stems, splits, samples, drops).

Each stage gets one page in the PDF: definition, pass/fail gates, one anonymized example.

### Delivery (the 3am test, in detail)

- **Storefront:** Gumroad (or Lemon Squeezy — better for non-US buyers). Reuses your existing OCD design tokens.
- **Page:** `ocdrecords.com/vault` — single sales page, no nav, three sections (the promise, the stack, the proof).
- **Fulfillment:** automated email from Gumroad with download links + a Typeform link for the Fast-Pass submission.
- **Fast-Pass:** Typeform collects the track URL + 3 questions; you record 8–10 min Looms once a month in a single 90-minute batch.
- **Total monthly time:** 90 min of Looms + ~30 min of inbox triage. That's it.

### Pricing logic

- **$197 single payment** — sits under the $200 impulse threshold, above the "treat it seriously" floor.
- **$147 founding-buyer price** — first 10 buyers from the *Transmission* list. Creates urgency and validates demand before public launch.
- **Order bumps:** $47 Stem Vol. 2 every 90 days; $97 Roster Deep-Dive video bundle. Bumps lift average order value by 20–30% with zero extra acquisition cost.
- **Don't** offer a payment plan. The audience can afford $197 if the offer is right, and plans triple your refund-and-chargeback overhead.

### What makes it irreplaceable

Three layers of moat, ranked by how hard they are to copy:

1. **Legal moat (hardest to copy):** the stems are literally from OCD's unreleased catalog sessions. No competitor can sell them.
2. **Mechanism moat:** the NPP is your internal rubric. Anyone can write *a* rubric — only OCD has *this* one, with this catalog as its training set.
3. **Proof moat:** the 12 anonymized scorecards inside the Vault are receipts. They show the rubric being applied to real submissions. Nobody else has this data.

If a competitor tried to clone this, they'd need to (a) sign artists, (b) accumulate years of A&R judgment, and (c) build a public reputation for taste. That's a five-year project. Yours is a 30-day project because you already did the five years.

## 5. Distribution — the channel you already own

You do **not** need a new audience. You need to convert the one you have.

### The submission funnel is your gold mine

Producers who submit to OCD are the most qualified leads in the world for this product. They are:

- already in the niche
- already familiar with the brand
- already asking to be judged

That last point is critical. The Vault literally answers the question they're already asking: *"Am I good enough?"*

### The three-touch system

1. **`submit.html` secondary CTA** — directly under the primary "Submit Your Work" button:
   > "Want the rubric we'll grade you against? Get The Artifact Vault →"
   This single placement is likely to convert at 2–5% of submission-page traffic. If you get 500 submissions a month, that's 10–25 Vault sales (~$2k–$5k MRR) from one button.

2. **Rejection email signature** — every "thanks but no" email ends with one line and a link. Rejections become revenue. This is the play Hormozi calls "monetize the no."

3. **Transmission newsletter feature** — one release cycle = one "Inside Entropy 001: the NPP scorecard" email. Reveal the rubric being applied to a real release. Soft CTA at the bottom. Treat this as content, not ad.

### Why no TikTok / no YouTube / no IG Reels

Three reasons:

- **You'd be starting from zero** on a platform whose algorithm rewards constant presence — the exact opposite of "productize yourself."
- **Your existing channel is higher intent.** A producer who submits a demo is 100× more qualified than a viewer who watched a 30-second hook.
- **The brand is dark, deliberate, considered.** It does not survive TikTok's tone. Don't dilute the worldview to fit a feed.

If you ever want to scale beyond what the funnel can produce, the next channel is **Splice / Sample Focus marketplace placement** for a free 10-stem teaser pack with the Vault upsell — not short-form video.

## 10. The honest assumptions I made — confirm before launch copy ships

I filled in the placeholders from the project. These are the four assumptions most worth sanity-checking:

1. **Buyer persona:** "underground electronic / cinematic producers, ages 22–38, already releasing on Bandcamp/SoundCloud, $50–$300/yr software spend." If the actual buyer is film/game composers, the offer pivots toward sync licensing and the price doubles.
2. **Build time:** 10–15 hrs/week solo. If you have a partner who can take the artist agreements and stem tagging, week 1 collapses to 3 days.
3. **Existing submission volume:** assumed 300–500/mo. If it's 50/mo, the funnel CTA still works but the *Transmission* newsletter has to carry more launch weight.
4. **Artist consent for stem rev share:** assumed willing. If even one anchor artist (Neural Ghost) says no, swap Vol. 1 to instrumentals from other roster members and ship a "Neural Ghost Vol. 2" later as the upsell.

If any of these are wrong, the *strategy* is the same — only the numbers and the launch sequence change.
